## Pilot script: "How Long Would You Last in the Ice Age?" (~9 min, ~1,350 words)

Voice: one calm man, curious tone, light humor. Cuts every 2-4 seconds. Text in [brackets] is the on-screen action.

[0:00 HOOK] Imagine you open your eyes and the air hurts to breathe. [stick guy in T-shirt, snow, wind lines] Around you: an ice wall taller than any building you have ever seen. [pull back to reveal glacier] Nobody is coming to help. The question is simple: how long would you last? [clock counts up] Let us find out, hour by hour.

[HOUR 1] You are standing in the Last Glacial Maximum, about twenty-five thousand years ago. The average temperature of the planet was several degrees colder than today, and in places like northern Europe it was far worse. [thermometer drops] You are wearing what you wore this morning. [T-shirt, jeans] Your hands go stiff first. Then your thoughts slow down. [hands turn blue] In the first hour, your body is already spending energy just to stay warm. [heat bar drops] You find no matches. No lighter. No phone signal. [phone: no signal] Verdict: you are in trouble, but you are not done yet.

[THE FIRST SECRET: CLOTHES] Here is what people back then knew that you do not. They did not just throw a fur over their shoulders. They sewed. Archaeologists have found bone needles tens of thousands of years old, with tiny eyes for thread. [needle close-up] Layers of animal skin, tailored to fit, trapping warm air next to the body. [figure dressing in layers] A sewn outfit was not fashion. It was the difference between waking up and not waking up. [stick guy shivers beside a sewing stick figure]

[HOUR 3: SHELTER] Shelter matters next. In some places, people built homes from mammoth bones, stacked like a puzzle, then covered them with hides. [mammoth-bone hut builds up] Elsewhere they used caves and rock shelters. A good shelter blocks wind, and wind is a silent killer. [wind arrows blocked by wall] You would not know how to build one. You do not have a mammoth.

[NIGHT 1: FIRE] Now night comes. And it is dark in a way you have probably never experienced. [screen fades to black] Fire is everything. It warms you, cooks your food, and keeps animals away. [campfire glow, eyes in the dark] But fire is not just a spark. You need dry fuel, in a land where wood is scarce and everything is frozen. [wet twigs, frost] People collected bones and fat to burn, and kept embers alive for days. [ember pot being carried] You? You will rub two sticks together for an hour and get a blister. [stick guy, smoke, blister] Verdict: you probably do not survive the first night. [clock: 12 hours]

[WHAT IF YOU WERE LUCKY] But say a hunting family finds you and takes you in. [group waving] Now the real work begins.

[DAY 3: FOOD] There are no supermarkets, so food has to be found, and it is not easy. In a frozen world, plants are scarce, so meat and fat matter a lot. [meat rack, fat chunk] Hunting a mammoth was not a lone act. It took a group, planning and patience. [group surrounds a mammoth] One mistake and a tusk can end your story. [stick guy dodges] You would be hungry by day three. You would be useful by never.

[WEEK 1: PREDATORS] Then the danger changes: other hunters. Cave lions, wolves, and hyenas shared this world. [eyes in the dark, shapes] People learned to move in groups, to keep fires lit, to tell stories about who not to meet. [campfire circle] You are not the top predator here. You are the snack. [stick guy with a funny sign: SNACK]

[MONTH 1: THE ODDLY GOOD NEWS] Now the surprising part. Life was not just suffering. People had tools, art, music, and friends. We have found flutes made from bone, carved figures, and painted caves. [flute, figurine, cave art] They were not just surviving. They were thinking, creating, and caring for each other. Some bones show people who were injured and still lived for years, which means someone looked after them. [bone with healed fracture, hand on shoulder]

[VERDICT] So how long would you last? Alone and unprepared: hours to a couple of days. [clock stops] With a group and a few lessons: maybe a lot longer. [group together] Because the secret of the Ice Age was never one tough guy. It was people who shared warmth, food and knowledge.

[CTA] If you want to know what they did when the fire went out, I will show you in the next video. [next-video card: FIRE DIED] Subscribe so you do not miss it, and tell me in the comments: how long do you think you would last?

## Fact-check list before recording (do not skip)
- Last Glacial Maximum date range and temperature difference: confirm with a source (e.g. a university or museum page).
- Bone needles: cite a named find (e.g. Denisova Cave needle) and age.
- Mammoth-bone dwellings: confirm site and age (e.g. Mezhyrich, Ukraine) and say "in some places".
- Cave lion and cave hyena range, bone flutes (e.g. Hohle Fels): confirm date.
- Healed injuries as evidence of care: pick a named example.
- Soften anything uncertain ("may have", "some archaeologists think").
- Put sources in the video description.
