## Fact check (web search, 2026-11-13; many results are news or popular-science pages)
| Claim in script | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Iron pyrite + flint make sparks; pyrite and heated flint tools found at Barnham, England, about 400,000 years ago | Supported | British Museum / Natural History Museum study, Nature (Dec 2025). Linked to early Neanderthals. Script says "researchers found... say people were making fire" |
| "That is long before the last Ice Age" | OK | 400,000 years is far older than the last glacial period |
| Manganese dioxide "may have helped"; scientists argue | Supported as debated | Heyes et al. 2016 (lowers ignition temperature about 100 C); Sorensen 2024 tests mixed; an EXARC study found no clear benefit |
| Tinder fungus "much later" | OK, hedged | Iceman (about 5,300 years ago) carried tinder fungus; fire use is inference |
| Hearths reused, swept, rebuilt at Ice Age sites | Supported | Hohle Fels and Gravettian sites (stacked hearths, ash layers) |
| Stone lamps burning animal fat; more than a hundred at Lascaux; possible juniper wick | Supported | French Ministry of Culture; world history references. Lamps are late Upper Paleolithic, Lascaux about 17,000 years |
| Carrying embers across snow; how is unknown | Hedged | No direct source found; script says "we do not know exactly how" and "may have". Keep hedged |
| Borrowing fire from a neighbouring camp, sharing fire "may have been" | Inference | Plausible, not documented. Hedged |
| Banking a coal under ash | General knowledge | Common firekeeping practice; not tied to a specific Ice Age find |
| Friction fire (drill) is hard; wet/frozen wood fails | General knowledge | Experimental archaeology and bushcraft; no Ice Age evidence claimed |
| Huddling, watch rota, animals at night | General / inference | Not sourced. Phrased as what people "do" |
| Fuel scarce on treeless tundra; bones/fat as fuel | Supported | See video 1 fact check |
