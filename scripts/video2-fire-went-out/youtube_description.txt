How long would you last when the fire goes out in the Ice Age?

You wake in the middle of a freezing night, and the fire is nearly dead. This video follows you hour by hour through what Ice Age people really did to keep fire alive: checking the ashes for a hidden ember, borrowing fire from neighbours, making sparks with flint and iron pyrite, spinning a stick for friction, and sharing warmth until morning.

This is an illustrated what-if scenario. The facts are based on archaeology: reused hearths at Ice Age sites, iron pyrite and heat-baked flint at Barnham, England (about 400,000 years ago), and stone fat lamps found at Lascaux. Where scientists disagree (such as the use of manganese dioxide to help a fire catch) the video says so.
