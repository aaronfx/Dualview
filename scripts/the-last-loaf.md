## Plain text (no tags)
One loaf. One knock. And a choice. Maria had not eaten since morning. Her little boy, Daniel, slept on the floor beside her, his stomach empty. She froze. And whispered, "Who would be out in a storm like this?" A hooded man stood in the rain. Soaked. Shaking. "Please... just a little bread." She looked at the loaf. She looked at her sleeping son. And softly, "Come in. Out of the rain." She broke the bread in two. Half for the stranger. Half for her boy. He ate slowly. A tear ran down his face. "Thank you, Maria." Maria went still. She had never told him her name. By morning, he was gone. But on her table sat a basket full of bread. And in the mud outside... footprints. Glowing gold. Whatever you give in love, God never forgets. If you needed this today, type Amen. And send it to someone who needs hope.

## How one man plays three people (acting notes)
- Narrator = the base voice, steady, low, unhurried.
- Maria's lines: same voice, but softer and slightly higher in the throat, quieter, more breath; do NOT do a woman's voice or a caricature. Make it sound like him remembering how she said it.
- The stranger's lines: lower, slower, hoarse, weak, almost no volume. Keep the sentences short.
- Keep the quotes intimate, as if told by a fireplace. The emotion lives in the pauses and the breath before "Come in".
- Biggest moments, in order: "Come in. Out of the rain." -> "Thank you, Maria." -> "She had never told him her name." Take 3 versions of each, pick the quietest one that still lands.
