# Story + title framework for animated faith shorts (emotional, encouraging, strong hook, body, CTA)

Use with `SKILL.md` (engine) and `reference-faith-in-god.md` (what is working on a similar channel). Our videos are ANIMATION (stick-figure doodle engine), so hooks must be drawable and readable in a small frame.

## 1. Title formulas (6-10 words, one emotion, one promise)
1. `[Small act of love], Then [God/Jesus answered]`  -> She Gave Her Last Bread to a Stranger. Then He Said Her Name.
2. `[Long wait / pain], Then [turning point]`  -> She Prayed for Two Years. Then Her Phone Rang.
3. `[Nobody ...], Then [one person/angel ...]`  -> Everyone Walked Past Him. One Child Stopped.
4. `If you [feel ...], watch this`  -> If You Feel Forgotten Today, Watch This.
5. `[They laughed/doubted], [God made it enough]`  -> They Laughed at His Small Gift. God Made It Enough.
6. Number + time + sudden shift  -> A Mother Prayed at 3 AM. God Answered Before Sunrise.
- Rules: first-person-free, concrete nouns (bread, coat, door, phone, candle), no vague words. One emotion: loneliness, hunger, waiting, forgiveness, hope. Never clickbait lies; the payoff must be delivered inside the video.
- Description: title + 🙏❤️ + 2-3 line plot summary + hashtag block (`#JesusIsKing #Amen #jesuslovesyou #JesusIsLord` plus 1-2 story tags like `#faith #prayer`). Label as a dramatized story ("inspired by Matthew 25:35-40" when scripture based).

## 3. Structure (45-60 s, about 2.5 words per second of narration = 110-140 words)
| Time | Part | Job | Animation notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 s | VISUAL HOOK | One striking, emotional image + one spoken line (<=10 words) + on-screen keyword | Empty chair + single candle; knock at the door in the rain; glowing footprints; last coin in a hand. Zoom push-in, sound/line lands on the first frame. Never start with a logo or slow establishing shot. |
| 3-8 s | SETUP | Who, what they lack, the stakes | One clear wide shot, character emotion readable (sad/worry). |
| 8-30 s | BODY (3 beats) | 1 need/pain -> 2 test/rejection (others walk away, doubt) -> 3 the costly act of love | One scene per beat, real interaction (hands, door, bread), cut/transition every 2-4 s, rising music. |
| 30-42 s | PAYOFF | Reveal + reward: golden light, angel, name spoken, phone rings, basket of bread | Biggest visual of the video; warm sky, glow, rays; hold 2 s without speech if possible. |
| 42-50 s | LANDING LINE | One quotable sentence / scripture echo | Slow zoom out, character at peace, text on screen. |
| 50-55 s | CTA | One ask only, warm not pushy | End card: "Type Amen" / "Share with someone who needs hope" + subscribe. |
- Emotional rules: show the sacrifice before the reward; let the viewer feel the cost (last loaf, last coin); the miracle answers the exact need set up in the first 8 s.
- Retention: a new image every 2-4 s, a question or tension at ~15 s ("Why did he know her name?"), payoff after 70% of runtime, loop-friendly ending that echoes the hook.

## 4. CTA bank (pick one per video)
- Type "Amen" if you believe God sees you.
- Share this with someone who needs hope today.
- Comment your prayer and let us pray with you.
- Follow for a new story of hope every day.
- Avoid guilt/superstition CTAs ("share in 10 seconds or lose your blessing"); keep it respectful.

## 5. Ready scripts (each about 45-50 s narrated)
### A. "She Gave Her Last Bread to a Stranger. Then He Said Her Name."
Hook (visual): rain at midnight, one loaf on a table, a knock. Text: "ONE LOAF."
"She had one loaf of bread left. And a stranger was asking for it. Maria hadn't eaten since morning. Her little boy was asleep, hungry. Outside, in the rain, a hooded man asked for food. She looked at the loaf. She looked at her son. And she opened the door. She broke the bread in two. Half for him. Half for her boy. The stranger ate slowly, with tears in his eyes. 'Thank you, Maria,' he whispered. She had never told him her name. By morning he was gone. But on her table sat a basket full of bread, and the footprints in the mud were glowing gold. Whatever you give in love, God never forgets. If you needed this today, type Amen, and share it with someone who needs hope."
Scenes: rain street, door opens, bread split, stranger's tear, name line (close-up), morning basket, glowing footprints, landing line, end card.

### B. "Nobody Came to His Birthday. Then Someone Knocked."
Hook (visual): table set for six, one candle, five empty chairs. Text: "NOBODY CAME."
"Old Samuel set the table for six. Six plates. Six chairs. Nobody came. Not his children. Not his friends. He blew out one candle, alone, and whispered, 'Lord, am I forgotten?' Then someone knocked. A young man in a plain white shirt stood at the door, holding a small gift. 'Happy birthday, Samuel.' Samuel did not know him. But when the stranger sat down, warm light filled the room, and peace filled every empty chair. For the first time in years, Samuel laughed. When he looked again, the stranger was gone. The gift was still there: an old Bible, open to one line. 'I will never leave you nor forsake you.' If you feel forgotten today, this is for you. Type Amen if you believe God sees you."

### C. "A Boy Shared His Only Lunch With Jesus" (John 6, five loaves and two fish)
Hook (visual): huge hungry crowd, one small boy with a tiny basket. Text: "FIVE LOAVES."
"Thousands of people were hungry. And one little boy had five loaves of bread and two fish. His friends laughed. 'That will not feed anyone.' But he walked forward anyway, and he gave everything he had to Jesus. Jesus thanked God, and began to break the bread. And the bread kept coming. And coming. Until every person had eaten, and twelve baskets were left over. The boy gave the little he had, and God made it more than enough. What small thing can you give today? Comment it below, and share this with someone who needs a reminder: little is a lot in God's hands."

## 6. Production checklist for each story
1. Write the narration (110-140 words), record it (ElevenLabs), send the mp3.
2. We cut scenes at the pauses, build 12-20 scenes, check hook is visible in frame 1.
3. Orientation by length (under 90 s vertical).
4. Title + description + hashtags from section 1; CTA end card.
5. Mark as fiction/dramatization; scripture-based stories cite the passage in the description.
