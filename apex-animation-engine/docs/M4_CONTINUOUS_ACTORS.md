# APEX M4 — Continuous Actors (first validated increment)

## User problem
Characters stopped to **notice**, stopped again to **act**, finally ran, and later
waited through a separate recovery before pursuing their goal. The earlier M3
film was physically correct but not decisive or entertaining.

## Changes (engine, not just editing video speed)
- `scene/schema.py`: a walking/running action may request a validated motion
  clip and `notice_while_moving` gaze target.
- `motion/controller.py`: start a noticed/acted movement and emit both
  causal events while grounded motion proceeds; consecutive runs retain gait
  segments; a forward `jump_to` may carry run-up speed instead of braking
  to zero first (but never reverse jumps or changes of support).
- `motion/animator.py`: the upper-body clip blends over an existing running
  gait. Root position, leg gait, planted feet and physical contacts remain
  controlled by the existing engine; no teleport or playback-speed trick.
- `scene/autochoreo.py`: **active** pacing now observes and emotes on the first
  run. **measured** retains the old staged notices/acting for comparison.
  Climb's active post-impact pursuit begins immediately with an overlay rather
  than waiting to complete an in-place recovery clip.
- `motion/clips/land_and_dash.json`: reusable original short
  impact-to-running expression (no bogus stationary knee contacts).
- `tests/test_autochoreo.py`: moving observation, acting and momentum,
  measured-mode compatibility, grounded contacts and brisk physical pickup.
- `.github/workflows/apex-m4-continuous-actors.yml`: regression, replay,
  native MP4 rendering and 42-check technical/cinematic QA; archives proof.

## Evidence
Last full verified run on commit `9af3fe93f`:
https://github.com/aaronfx/Dualview/actions/runs/45297945802

The video was generated by actual APEX at 960x540, H.264, 24 fps, 196
frames, 8.1667 seconds. The test workflow passed 22 tests and **42/42
technical/cinematic QA checks**. The physics planner chose
`climb_then_drop` and real recorded events included:
- `notice` / first acting performance: t=0.000s while moving
- `climbed`: t=4.0083s
- `takeoff`: t=4.2708s
- `impact`: t=5.2375s
- physical pickup of the real reward: t=6.5000s

Comparison to immediately preceding active M3 benchmark:
- film duration: 10.88 → **8.17 seconds** (~25% shorter).
- pickup: 9.2167 → **6.5000 seconds** (~2.72 s earlier).
- all 42 QA checks still pass.

These are *pacing* improvements, **not** a claim of achieving professional
artistic/reference-film quality. A reviewer must inspect the real MP4.

## Remaining, intentionally not overclaimed
- Climbing still spends visually noticeable time approaching and bracing
  against the barrier. Needs contact-aware action continuity and physical
  climb redesign.
- Observation/reaction is an upper-body overlay, not a general autonomous
  actor with online replanning.
- Only the existing bounded one-character M3 world was demonstrated.
- No general scene-wide boredom detection, many-character conversations or
  learned motion generation yet. No deployment and no merge into main.

## Reproduction
From `apex-animation-engine` after installing `.[dev]` plus FFmpeg:

```bash
python -m pytest -q tests/test_autochoreo.py tests/test_motion_performance_probe.py tests/test_route_planner.py tests/test_motion.py
python examples/build_impossible_crossing.py out/m4/impossible_crossing_m4.json
apex validate out/m4/impossible_crossing_m4.json
apex render out/m4/impossible_crossing_m4.json -o out/m4/impossible_crossing_m4.mp4 --scale 0.75 --samples 1 --qa-json out/m4/qa.json --review-dir out/m4/review
```
