# APEX M4 — Continuous Actors (first validated increment)

## User problem
Characters stopped to **notice**, stopped again to **act**, finally ran, and later
waited through a separate recovery before pursuing their goal. The earlier M3
film was physically correct but not decisive or entertaining.

## Changes (engine, not just editing video speed)
- `scene/schema.py`: a walking/running action may request a validated motion
  clip and `notice_while_moving` gaze target.
- `motion/controller.py`: start a noticed/acted movement and emit both
  causal events while grounded motion proceeds; consecutive runs retain gait
  segments; a forward `jump_to` may carry run-up speed instead of braking
  to zero first (but never reverse jumps or changes of support).
- `motion/animator.py`: the upper-body clip blends over an existing running
  gait. Root position, leg gait, planted feet and physical contacts remain
  controlled by the existing engine; no teleport or playback-speed trick.
- `scene/autochoreo.py`: **active** pacing now observes and emotes on the first
  run. **measured** retains the old staged notices/acting for comparison.
  Climb's active post-impact pursuit begins immediately with an overlay rather
  than waiting to complete an in-place recovery clip.
- `motion/clips/land_and_dash.json`: reusable original short
  impact-to-running expression (no bogus stationary knee contacts).
- `tests/test_autochoreo.py`: moving observation, acting and momentum,
  measured-mode compatibility, grounded contacts and brisk physical pickup.
- `.github/workflows/apex-m4-continuous-actors.yml`: regression, replay,
  native MP4 rendering and 42-check technical/cinematic QA; archives proof.

## Remaining, intentionally not overclaimed
- Climbing still spends visually noticeable time approaching and bracing
  against the barrier. Needs contact-aware action continuity and physical
  climb redesign.
- Observation/reaction is an upper-body overlay, not a general autonomous
  actor with online replanning.
- Only the existing bounded one-character M3 world was demonstrated.
- No general scene-wide boredom detection, many-character conversations or
  learned motion generation yet. No deployment and no merge into main.

## Increment 2 — run-to-climb continuity and wasted-time audit (2026-11-24)

Verified build: https://github.com/aaronfx/Dualview/actions/runs/45300052680
(sha `24379c797`; subsequent docs-only commits do not modify this executable build).

**Root cause.** The actor previously stopped at a generic point some
distance from the barrier, and the climb controller then scheduled a second
approach to its exact ledge-grab position. After that, its timed climb included
a hanging hold. Shortening these phases exposed a second defect: the camera
editor assumed the old slow action schedule, producing a sub-0.5s shot.
We corrected the shot timeline rather than slowing the performer.

**Changes.**
- The planner obtains the actor's real leg proportions and computes the same
  grab-start coordinate as the climb controller: edge minus 30% of standing
  height. The controller carries velocity into a *matching* climb approach.
- A tempo-bounded, contact-preserving climb shortens reach/ledge-grab/mantle
  phases together; the hand-IK targets, causal ledge event and support change
  are retained. APEX's climb is still an authored kinematic motion, **not** a
  physically simulated two-hand grasp with force feedback.
- Event-driven shot boundaries adapt to the earlier climb and keep the
  cinematic gate's minimum shot duration.
- `qa/actor_pacing.py` scans actual 240 Hz root trajectories and action
  segments for wasted idle and movement stalls. Dramatic acting, purposeful
  holds and physical recovery remain visible but excluded from the waste
  budget; it cannot decide whether a deliberate hold is aesthetically good.
- `examples/audit_m4_actor_pacing.py` exports `actor_pacing_report.json`
  before rendering, with a hard threshold of 0.65s wasted time.

**Evidence.** 50 Python tests passed; actual APEX MP4 is 960x540 H.264
with AAC, 24 fps, 167 frames, 6.9583s. Film QA: **42 PASS, 0 FAIL**,
technical PASS and cinematic PASS. Six planner tactics were evaluated;
`climb_then_drop` passed and physically collected the specified reward at
t=5.2958s. The 8.1667s previous M4 version was 1.2084s longer; this is
about a 14.8% reduction in film duration on the *same scene*.

The idle audit reported **0.40s of unmotivated final idle**, **0.875s of
explicit celebration performance**, and an accepted 0.65s waste limit.
The remaining idle is real and should be reduced by the editor; do not count
the audit PASS as a human artistic acceptance. The climb movement still
looks fairly mechanical relative to the three reference films.

**Next.** Remove remaining non-dramatic end holds without truncating the
payoff; generalize live act blending to turns, grabs and multi-character
interaction; validate in three *different* original scenes, not just this
single obstacle benchmark. Final artistic review remains outstanding.


## Owner-detected ghost-through defect — corrected 2026-11-24

**What failed visually.** In the 6.96s M4 video, IKO ran straight
through the small silver crate before climbing the main barrier.
The 42/42 general animation QA checks did **not** detect this.

**Root cause (verified in actual output JSON):** The crate was configured
`collide_characters: false` with a foreground depth lane (`z: 55`)
while still looking like a solid object. The choreography planner ignored
the prop when generating the `climb_then_drop` path.

**Corrections:** The crate is collidable in the actor's lane; the plan
now includes a physical jump over it before the main climb. Camera cuts
select the main platform's last impact, not the earlier crate landing.
Regression tests require two distinct jumps and verify the actor's
240Hz feet clearance above the moving crate's top, not just success
collecting the reward.

**Evidence:** GitHub Actions proof
https://github.com/aaronfx/Dualview/actions/runs/45301392865
passed 17 tests and 42/42 video QA. Native output: 7.62s, 183 frames,
960x540. Frame inspection confirms a visible hop at ~0.77–1.73s before
the main climb. The longer duration is necessary physical storytelling,
not a regression in acting speed.

**Remaining general QA gap:** Add scene-wide rendered-space ghosting
checks for props, including noncolliding set dressing that visually
looks like a solid obstacle. A passing 42/42 general gate is not proof
that *all* object interactions look plausible.
